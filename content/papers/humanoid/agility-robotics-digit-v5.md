## Summary

Agility Robotics detailed its next-generation Digit V5 humanoid, designed to work alongside humans without safety fencing, targeting 20-hour operational days, standardized (eventually self-swapping) end-effectors, and roughly 50lb lift capacity, with early-customer deployment targeted for December 2026. The announcement coincides with Agility's roughly $2.5B SPAC merger to go public, backed by $300M in booked revenue.

## Key Contributions

- Positions Digit V5 specifically around operating without traditional safety fencing ("out of the cage"), directly addressing the functional-safety certification gap for humanoids highlighted elsewhere in this digest's research (see "Toward Certified Functional Safety for Industrial Humanoid Robots").
- 20-hour operational days and standardized end-effectors target the practical, unglamorous requirements of continuous industrial shift work rather than headline capability demos.
- A concrete near-term commercial deployment date (December 2026) and reported booked revenue ($300M) distinguish this from purely research-stage humanoid announcements.

## Strengths

- The focus on operational endurance, safety-fence-free operation, and swappable end-effectors reflects requirements grounded in actual warehouse/factory deployment constraints, rather than optimizing for demo-friendly but less commercially relevant capabilities.
- Booked revenue and a public listing represent an unusual level of commercial validation for a humanoid robotics company relative to peers still primarily in a research/demo phase.

## Weaknesses

- No official Agility Robotics blog post or technical disclosure was found accompanying this announcement — available coverage is entirely secondary press reporting (Forbes, GeekWire), so specific technical claims (safety certification status, actual measured endurance, task success rates) are not independently verifiable at time of writing.
- "Work alongside humans without safety fencing" is a strong safety claim that, per the concurrently published Siemens feasibility study on humanoid fail-passive certification gaps, remains a genuinely unresolved certification challenge industry-wide — it's unclear from press coverage what safety case Agility is relying on to support fence-free operation.

## Open Questions

- What specific safety certification or engineering approach does Digit V5 use to justify fence-free operation, given the broader industry-wide fail-passive certification gap for legged humanoids?
- Will the December 2026 early-customer deployment date hold, and what task success/reliability metrics will be disclosed once real deployments begin?

## Significance

A significant commercial milestone for humanoid robotics — a public listing backed by booked revenue is a different maturity signal than the research-stage announcements that dominate most of this vault — but the safety and technical claims warrant follow-up once primary-source technical documentation becomes available.
