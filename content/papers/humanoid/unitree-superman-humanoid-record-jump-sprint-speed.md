## Summary

Unitree unveiled a new humanoid robot nicknamed "Superman," claiming a roughly 2-meter standing vertical jump and a 12.66 m/s sprint speed — both exceeding official human world records — developed in just over three months. The unveiling coincided with Unitree's Shanghai STAR Market IPO. No price, mass-production timeline, or target customer segment has been disclosed.

## Key Contributions

- If accurate, the claimed jump height and sprint speed would represent a substantial leap in dynamic locomotion capability over previously reported humanoid performance figures.
- The reported three-month development timeline, if accurate, would suggest rapid iteration on Unitree's existing humanoid locomotion stack rather than a from-scratch redesign.

## Strengths

- Extreme dynamic locomotion capability (jumping, sprinting) stress-tests balance, actuator power density, and control-loop performance in ways that stationary or slow-walking demonstrations do not, making it a meaningful (if narrow) capability signal if verified.

## Weaknesses

- This is a hardware showcase announcement timed with an IPO, not a peer-reviewed technical disclosure — there is no published methodology, controlled test conditions description, or independent verification of the claimed figures at time of writing.
- Independent analysts quoted in press coverage note that controlled lab/demo results for dynamic locomotion often do not translate directly to reliable, repeatable real-world or mass-production performance — a caution that applies directly here given the total absence of deployment or reliability data.
- No information on payload capacity, task-relevant capability, or how (if at all) these locomotion feats connect to the whole-body manipulation and task-execution capabilities that matter for most humanoid commercial use cases.

## Open Questions

- Under what test conditions (surface, safety rigging, number of successful attempts vs. failures) were the jump and sprint figures measured?
- Does "Superman's" locomotion capability translate into any task-relevant benefit for Unitree's commercial humanoid roadmap, or is it primarily a capability/marketing demonstration timed with the IPO?

## Significance

Notable primarily as a capability and marketing signal from a major humanoid hardware manufacturer around a significant corporate milestone (IPO) — should be read as an unverified hardware claim pending independent testing or a technical disclosure, consistent with how other hardware-showcase announcements in this vault (e.g. Figure 03's ladder climb) are best treated.
